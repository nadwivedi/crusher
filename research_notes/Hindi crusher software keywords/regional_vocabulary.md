# Regional Hindi/Hinglish Crusher Vocabulary (Hindi belt, 2026)

Method note: Most evidence comes from Hindi news reports (Patrika, Amar Ujala, Prabhat Khabar, Zee News Hindi, Udaipur Times) and IndiaMART/ExportersIndia seller listings. Searches in Devanagari through the search tool often returned irrelevant results. YouTube, Facebook groups and Quora were not reachable in a useful way, so I could not verify spoken or colloquial words (munshi, khalasi, udhari, bilty) from primary sources. Those words are marked as uncertain.

## 1. Local words for crushed aggregate and sizes, by region

### Takeaway
**गिट्टी (gitti)** is the main word in MP, Chhattisgarh, UP (east and central), Bihar and Rajasthan news. **रोड़ी (rodi)** is the Haryana, Delhi NCR and Uttarakhand word. **Stone chips / स्टोन चिप्स** is the Jharkhand (Pakur, Palamu) word. Fines are called **डस्ट / स्टोन डस्ट** almost everywhere, and in MP also **धूला चूरा**. **बजरी (bajri)** has two meanings: crushed stone in Haryana and Gurugram ("jeera bajri", "reta bajri"), but sand or gravel in Rajasthan and Uttarakhand.

### Inferences
- Approximate primary-word map (high confidence where cited above):
  - **गिट्टी (gitti):** MP, CG, UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, and also Jharkhand alongside chips
  - **रोड़ी (rodi/rori):** Haryana, Delhi NCR, and likely western UP and Uttarakhand (Uttarakhand and western UP were not directly confirmed by the fetched articles; this is uncertain)
  - **स्टोन चिप्स (chips):** Jharkhand (Pakur, Palamu); likely also Bihar trade, which buys Pakur chips (uncertain)
  - **डस्ट / स्टोन डस्ट:** everywhere; **धूला चूरा** is used in MP; **चूरा/चूरी** probably appears elsewhere (uncertain)
  - **बजरी:** crushed fines or grit in Haryana and Gurugram ("jeera bajri") and in Bharatpur; **sand** in Bhilwara (Rajasthan, where "bajri" is the standard word for river sand) and Uttarakhand ("रेत-बजरी")
  - **मौरंग/मोरंग:** coarse river sand in UP, not aggregate; it appears in the same rate lists as gitti
- Size naming: both mm sizes (10 एमएम, 20 एमएम, 40 एमएम) and "jeera" (10 mm) are used. MP uses mm sizes in official crusher association rates.
- I found no source evidence for **kapchi**, **khanda**, **roda**, or **metal** in Hindi-belt pages in this session. From my own knowledge (uncertain): "kapchi" is mainly Gujarati and Marathi-border usage, "khanda" or "boulder" refers to large raw stone feed, and "metal" or "gitti metal" is an older road-construction term (e.g. "40 mm metal"). The report writer should treat these as unverified.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on how common rodi is versus gitti in western UP (Meerut, Saharanpur) and Uttarakhand crusher zones.
- I could not reach YouTube titles, Facebook groups or Quora Hindi to confirm spoken spellings.
- The meaning of "मिलर" in Bhilwara was not confirmed.

## 3. Measurement units by region

### Takeaway
The unit changes by region. **Cubic feet ("फीट", "घन फीट", "सीएफटी")** and **per truck** are used in UP, Chhattisgarh and Bihar. **Cubic metre ("घन मीटर")** is used in official and association rates in MP. **Ton ("टन")** is used in Haryana, NCR and Jharkhand seller listings. **Trolley/tractor** loads are the retail unit in Rajasthan and UP. **Brass** (100 cu ft) appears in Chhattisgarh seller listings but did not appear in any Hindi news article I found.

### Inferences
- Software needs to support several units at once: ton (from the weighbridge), cubic feet / "feet", cubic metre (MP royalty and eTP) and brass, plus per-trip/per-vehicle pricing (truck, hywa, trolley). Conversion between weight and volume is a real pain point, since one article treats "500 फीट" and "20 टन" as the same truckload.
- "फीट" or "फुट" alone means cubic feet in everyday speech, and news even writes "वर्गफीट". Keyword pages should use "फीट" and "CFT" rather than only "cubic feet".
- Brass usage in the Hindi belt is probably limited to CG and MP areas near Maharashtra (uncertain; supported by only one Raipur listing).

### Gaps
- No Hindi source says how many cubic feet or tonnes a "hyva bhar" is, or what a standard trolley holds, by region.
- I could not confirm whether MP crushers use brass in day-to-day speech.

The strongest candidates, based on confirmed real-world use: **क्रशर/क्रेशर + हिसाब/सॉफ्टवेयर**, **गिट्टी** (with state names: MP, CG, UP, Bihar, Rajasthan), **रोड़ी** (Haryana/NCR), **स्टोन चिप्स** (Jharkhand), **रवन्ना/ई-रवन्ना** (Rajasthan, Uttarakhand), **eTP/ई-टीपी** (MP), **चालान** (Bihar), **रॉयल्टी**, **तुलाई कांटा/कांटा पर्ची**, **हाइवा**, **डंपर**, **ट्रैक्टर-ट्रॉली**, **डस्ट**, and unit words: **घन मीटर, फीट/CFT, ब्रास, टन**.
